Because each batch investigates model behavior under specific prompts, board representations, and session protocols, their numbers cannot be merged into a single cross-game, cross-effort leaderboard.

## How to Read Experimental Conditions & Sample Sizes

All entries below record the exact conditions used when each batch was run, without retroactively rewriting historical experiments to match current defaults:
- **Session modes:** `stateless` starts a fresh session for every move; `session` maintains a continuous multi-turn conversation; `notes` passes only a self-written text note to the next turn.
- **Match protocols:** `real` is the **"Model as Real Player"** protocol (no check warnings, threat lists, or legal-move enumerations); early batches used legacy scaffolding and are not equivalent to `real`. In full games, standard initial positions and randomized book/engine openings (where the harness plays the first $N$ plies) must also be distinguished.
- **Model identifiers:** We list the exact model strings confirmed by in-repo artifacts. Where a report recorded only the alias `claude:opus`, this index preserves that alias; the exact identifier `claude-opus-5-5` for the 14 old-CLI Chess games (Opus vs. Astra) and the 30-game rating calibration was confirmed by a [subsequent historical audit](results/argon-high-chess-rating-20261019.md) and is not extrapolated to other batches. `agy:claude-opus-5-5-high` and the old CLI's `claude-opus-5-5` at different effort settings do not represent identical compute budgets.
- **Sample units:** "Positions", "responses", and "games" are stated separately. Repeated runs on the same position, the two sides of the same game, and games sharing identical opening sequences are not independent samples. Blunder thresholds and mate-in-$N$ scoring criteria also differ between International Chess and Xiangqi—check the original report definitions before comparing across tables. Re-prompting after safety refusals in older reports reflects historical handling; since 2026-10-15, the rule is to halt immediately on any anti-CoT-extraction refusal (see [Project Status (zh-CN)](../STATUS.md)).

## Batches 1–3: Reasoning Depth, Carryover & Prompting (2026-09-25 to 09-27)

- [Batch 1A: Reasoning Depth](results/batch1-depth.md) (09-26, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, Claude CLI, `stateless`; `medium` / `high` / `xhigh` / `max`. 30 positions per game × 2 passes per effort = 60 responses per game per effort (480 responses total). Higher effort levels perform substantially better on the selected benchmark; this measures tactical quiz accuracy rather than full-game Elo, and the position bank carries selection bias toward prior failure points.

- [Batch 1B: Thinking Carryover](results/batch1-carryover.md) (09-25 to 09-26, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, `high`; `stateless` / `notes` / `session`. 3 games per mode against Maia-1100 in Chess and 2 games per mode against Fairy-Stockfish (`nodes=100`) in Xiangqi (15 games total, model always playing first), plus 37 calculation sessions and 6 move-selection sessions testing carryover (213 calls total). Confirms that short-interval session continuation genuinely reuses prior thinking blocks, but session continuity alone does not eliminate blunders in full games.

- [Batch 2 Block 0: Chinese Prompting](results/batch2-0.md) (09-26, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, `xhigh`, `stateless`; 30 positions per game × 3 passes = 180 responses, compared against 60 responses per game from Batch 1. Adding an explicit Chinese-response instruction reduced English replies to 3.3%; the concurrent rise in Chess solve rate cannot be attributed solely to prompt language because the test crossed days and minor CLI versions.

- [Batch 2A: Capture Relations & Mate Vision](results/batch2-capture.md) (09-26, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, `xhigh`, `stateless`, Chinese; `base` / `selfcheck` / `threats` / `legal`. 30 Xiangqi positions × 3 passes and 21 Chess positions × 2 passes = 90 / 42 responses per condition (528 total). Asking the model to list threats first reduces narrow piece-hanging blunders but does not reliably lower overall blunder rates; providing legal moves is a legacy assisted condition and is not part of the `real` protocol. (Includes position `x2-25`, which was excluded in later batches.)

- [Batch 2 B1 / C: Chess Strength & Session Continuity](results/batch2-chess-strength.md) (09-26). `claude-opus-5-5`, `xhigh`; B1 `stateless` (16 games vs. random, Maia-1500, and Maia-1900); C `session` vs. Maia-1900 (8 games paired by opening seed and color); 8-ply random opening book. Scored 70% across 10 games against Maia-1900 (affected by draws from winning positions and small sample size); quality gains from `session` over `stateless` did not reach the preset significance threshold. Note: "Maia-1900" is a model name, not a 1900 human rating.

- [Batch 2 B2: Xiangqi Strength](results/batch2-xiangqi-strength.md) (Batch 2). Opus (exact version string omitted in report), `xhigh`, `stateless`, Chinese; 6-ply random opening. 8 games each against Fairy-Stockfish `nodes=30` and `nodes=100` (16 games total), plus 5 engine-vs-engine calibration pairs (20 games each). Places the model on the ultra-low-node Fairy-Stockfish ladder without converting to human ranks.

- [Batch 3A: Writing Visible Rationales](results/batch3-reason.md) (09-26 to 09-27, both games). Opus, `xhigh`, `stateless`, Chinese; `base` vs. `reason`. 29 Xiangqi positions × 3 passes and 21 Chess positions × 2 passes = 87 / 42 responses per condition (258 total). Writing a rationale before the move makes it possible to diagnose roughly half of blunders from a specific false statement in the text, while neither improving nor degrading move quality.

- [Batch 3B: Max-Effort Full Games (Halted Early)](results/batch3-maxdepth.md) (09-27, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, `max`, `stateless`; same random openings and engine opponents as Batch 2. Planned 16 games, halted after 4 Chess and 2 Xiangqi games due to extreme quota cost (remaining 10 voided). The model won all 6 completed games with lower average centipawn loss (ACPL), at 3–8.5× the per-call cost of `xhigh`.

- [Round 1 Summary](results/summary-round1.md) (09-27, synthesizing 09-25 to 09-27, both games). Synthesizes Batches 1–3 and incorporates Batch 4 board presentation results (174 responses per main condition in Xiangqi, 126 in Chess), without adding new games. Documents the Phase 1 takeaway that **reasoning depth dominates while session carryover and prompt tricks are secondary**. Does not cover the later `real` protocol, Astra, Argon, or the 30/37-game Max rating calibration.

- [Rating & Human Population Reference](results/rating-reference.md) (09-27, corrected 10-03; Chess, with Go and Xiangqi notes). Literature survey with no new model games; updated on 10-03 to incorporate the 30-game Opus Max calibration. Explicitly invalidates the early `Maia-1900 ≈ Lichess 1900` assumption (Maia-1900's empirical Lichess Blitz rating against humans is ~1610) and explains why Xiangqi lacks a reliable human-scale anchor.

## Batches 4–6: Board Presentation & Tool Isolation Verification (2026-09-27 to 09-28)

- [Batch 4: How to Present the Board](results/batch4-presentation.md) (09-27, both games). `claude-opus-5-5`, `high`, `stateless`, Chinese; `all` / `board` / `pieces` / `fen`, plus follow-up `boardpieces`. 29 Xiangqi and 21 Chess positions; 2 initial passes (400 responses) plus 4 follow-up passes on key conditions (174 responses each for Xiangqi `all` / `board` / `pieces`, 116 for `boardpieces`; 126 each for Chess `all` / `board`). More information is not always better: in Xiangqi, giving **only the piece list (`pieces`)** performed best (21% blunder rate vs. 34% for `all`, and avoided confusing Black's `車` with Red's on the ASCII grid); in Chess, `board` and `all` (`FEN + ASCII board`) were indistinguishable, and `FEN + ASCII board` was retained because a bare board omits castling and en passant rights.

- [Batch 5: Cross-Model Presentation Retest](results/batch5-cross-model-presentation.md) (09-28, both games). `claude-opus-5-5` / `gpt-6-sol` / `deepseek-v4-pro`, all `high`, `stateless`, Chinese; 29 / 21 positions. Reuses Batch 4 for Opus; Sol ran 174 responses per condition in Xiangqi (3 conditions) and 42 in Chess (3 conditions); DeepSeek ran 54 / 56 / 57 in Xiangqi and 37 / 17 / 11 in Chess. Supports keeping the shared defaults (Xiangqi `pieces`, Chess `FEN + board`), though the data is insufficient for absolute cross-model ranking (DeepSeek has missing positions due to quota exhaustion, Codex carried user-level global rules in context, and engine re-scoring has minor hash jitter).

- [Batch 5 Prep: Tool Isolation](results/batch5-isolation.md) (09-28, integration check, no full games). `gpt-6-sol` and `deepseek-v4-pro`, Codex CLI 0.157.1, explicit `high`, `stateless` / `notes`; local request proxy capture plus 1 live adversarial prompt each. Confirmed 0 tools in the outgoing API request payload under that configuration.

- [Batch 6: Presentation Retest Under the Real-Player Protocol](results/batch6-real-presentation.md) (09-28, both games). Opus (Claude CLI) and `gpt-6-sol`, `high`, `stateless`, `real` protocol, no rationale; 29 / 21 positions (387 Opus responses, 174 Sol responses). Supports retaining the default representations (`pieces` for Xiangqi, `FEN + board` for Chess) under `--protocol real` without observed degradation in move legality on these samples; this is not a universal proof that omitting check alerts never has an effect (and comparisons against earlier batches are not strictly same-condition).

- [`llm-chat` Three-Backend Isolation & Thinking Carryover](results/llm-chat-isolation.md) (09-28, integration check). Claude `opus` / `gpt-6-sol` / `deepseek-v4-pro`; 17 live calls plus local stub verification. Confirmed zero tools for Claude and DeepSeek; GPT requests via Codex carried 4 disabled code-mode tool schemas whose execution layer is blocked (and `agi_chess` additionally audits session logs after every turn). Both Claude and GPT carry thinking across turns in `llm-chat`, whereas DeepSeek carries only visible response text.

## Full Games Under the Real-Player Protocol & Failure Diagnosis (Late Sep – Early Oct 2026)

All full games below start from the standard initial board position, use independent continuous sessions per player, and run under the `real` protocol via `llm-chat` (Xiangqi: piece list; Chess: FEN + ASCII board). Except for the initial Opus–Sol smoke pair, all games require a visible rationale before each move. Because openings often repeat across small samples and both players' ACPL fluctuates together with game complexity, raw match scores cannot be converted directly into Elo differences.

- [Opus vs. Sol: First Full Games](results/game-opus-vs-sol-1.md) (Batch `g1`; both games). `claude-opus-5-5` vs. `gpt-6-sol`, both `xhigh`, Opus playing first; 1 game per chess variant (2 games total), no rationale required, up to 4 illegal attempts per turn. Opus won both by checkmate; primarily validates the end-to-end `real` match loop.

- [Why In-Session Thinking Volume Is Lower](results/thinking-in-session.md) (diagnosing `g1` Chess). Opus and Sol, `xhigh`; 12 positions (6 per player) tested across 4 conditions (`session`, fresh session, legacy path, legacy protocol; 43 live calls per model). Shows that lower thinking token counts in full games stem primarily from multi-turn session continuation and familiar opening/lopsided endgame phases rather than a bug in effort passing, with no observed drop in move quality.

- [Opus vs. Astra: International Chess (Both `xhigh`)](results/game-opus-vs-astra-1.md) (09-29, 3 rounds, `g2-c-*`). `claude:opus` (`claude-opus-5-5`) vs. `gpt-6-astra`, both `xhigh`, thinking retained, brief rationale; 6 games, Opus 2:4 Astra. ACPL was 61.0 (Opus) vs. 55.0 (Astra) with 23 vs. 19 Lichess blunders—much closer in move quality than the 2:4 scoreline suggests.

- [Opus vs. Astra: Xiangqi (Both `xhigh`)](results/game-opus-vs-astra-xq-1.md) (Batches `g3` / `g4`, `g3-x-*`, `g4-x-*`). `claude:opus` vs. `gpt-6-astra`, both `xhigh`, thinking retained; 4 games, Opus 4:0 Astra. ACPL was 134.5 vs. 177.7. Both models repeatedly suffered from the same blind spot—walking a Rook behind their own Cannon into a discovered/screened Cannon capture (`炮隔炮打车`) and failing to capture the hanging Rook for up to 9 consecutive plies.

- [Opus `xhigh` vs. Astra `max`: International Chess](results/game-opus-vs-astra-max-1.md) (Batches `g3` / `g5`, `g3-c-max-*`, `g5-c-max-*`). `claude:opus` (`xhigh`) vs. `gpt-6-astra` (`max`); 4 games, 2:2. Both players' ACPL dropped together (35.7 vs. 37.1, 6 blunders each), showing that cross-batch ACPL shifts cannot be attributed to one side's effort change alone.

- [Opus `xhigh` vs. Astra `max`: Xiangqi](results/game-opus-vs-astra-max-xq-1.md) (Batch `g5`, `g5-x-max-*`). `claude:opus` (`xhigh`) vs. `gpt-6-astra` (`max`); 4 games, 2:2 (compared to 4:0 when both were `xhigh`). Astra's relative ACPL gap narrowed from +32% to +8% (71.9 vs. 66.7), directionally supporting that `max` effort helps Astra in Xiangqi.

- [Missed Captures & Uncaptured Hanging Pieces](results/missed-captures.md) (09-29 to 09-30, both games). Opus and `gpt-6-astra`; 14 failure positions forked from truncated session logs across the 17 `g2`–`g5` games, re-queried 4–6 times per cell. In 13 of 14 cases, the model's visible rationale never mentioned the critical capture—yet when asked in isolation to "list all legal captures", the model listed every single one correctly 26 out of 26 times, pointing to **selective attention during search rather than board-reading or rule ignorance** on these 14 targeted failure positions (this is a failure-point diagnosis and cannot be generalized to all board positions, nor does visible rationale equal hidden thinking). Prompt tweaks had little effect; for Opus, **dropping prior thinking blocks (`drop_thinking`)** when continuing the session improved capture rate on 3 of the 7 cases (from 16/84 to 44/84 overall).

- [Opus (`drop_thinking`) vs. Astra (`xhigh`)](results/game-opus-drop-vs-astra-1.md) (09-30, Batch `g6-*`, both games). `claude:opus` (`xhigh` with `drop_thinking=True`) vs. `gpt-6-astra` (`xhigh`), 4 games per variant (8 games total); Opus scored 3:1 in Chess and 4:0 in Xiangqi. Dropping prior thinking reduced Opus's missed-capture plies, at the cost of 2–3× higher per-move thinking tokens and cost. Kept as an experimental finding without changing the fair-play default (both sides retaining thinking).

- [Peak Showdown: Opus `max` vs. Astra `max`](results/game-opus-max-vs-astra-max-1.md) (09-30 to 10-01, Batch `g7-*`, both games). `claude:opus` (`claude-opus-5-5`) vs. `gpt-6-astra`, both `max`, thinking retained, `plain` rationale; 4 games per variant (8 games total). Opus won 3:1 in Chess (ACPL 47.0 vs. 58.0) and 4:0 in Xiangqi (ACPL 54.8 vs. 86.3; blunders 3 vs. 12). Upgrading Opus from `xhigh` to `max` against Astra `max` shifted the combined series score from 4:4 to 7:1.

## Rating Calibration Against Engines (2026-10-01 to 10-04)

- [Opus Max Rating Calibration](results/opus-max-rating.md) (10-01 to 10-03 matches, 10-04 adjudication update; both games, `r-c-*` and `r-x-*`). `claude-opus-5-5`, `max`, `llm-chat`, continuous session with thinking, `plain` rationale, `real` protocol; 8-ply (Chess) and 6-ply (Xiangqi) paired random openings.
  - **International Chess (30 games):** Played against Maia-1900 and three strength-limited Stockfish 19 levels (`sf1900`, `sf2050`, `sf2200`). Anchored to empirical Lichess ratings of the official Maia bots: under the original rule (200-ply cap = draw), Opus Max fits at **+281 scale points above Maia-1900** (95% CI: 173–392), equivalent to **~1910 Lichess Blitz / ~2020 Lichess Rapid**; under the 10-04 engine-adjudication rule for 200-ply cap games (where ≥300 cp advantage wins), 7 of the 8 capped games were adjudicated as losses, yielding **+189 above Maia-1900** (95% CI: 66–313), equivalent to **~1800 Lichess Blitz / ~1920 Lichess Rapid** (roughly top 15–23% Blitz / top 5–10% Rapid on Lichess, or ~1750–1840 FIDE).
  - **Xiangqi (37 games):** Played against a Fairy-Stockfish 14.0.1 node ladder (`nodes=30, 100, 300, 1000`). Fits at **+121 Elo above `nodes=100`** (95% CI: +1 to +236), comparable to or slightly above `nodes=300` (+82) and clearly below `nodes=1000` (+385). No human rank conversion is claimed for Xiangqi.
  - All rating scales can be recomputed offline via `python3 analysis/rating/rate.py report`; all 67 games' `game.pgn` files (with per-move rationales, timing, and token counts) and `analysis.jsonl` engine evaluations are checked into `runs/`.

- [Argon High vs. agy Opus 5.5 High: Game 1](results/argon-vs-agy-opus-high-20261019.md) (10-19, Chess, `runs/argon-vs-agy-opus-high-20261019`). `gemini:gemini-4-argon-high` vs. `agy:claude-opus-5-5-high`, both `high` via `llm-chat → agy`, standard opening; 1 game (75 plies, Argon won as White; ACPL 18.3 vs. 43.5).

- [Argon High vs. agy Opus 5.5 High: Three-Game Batch & Four-Game Combined Analysis](results/argon-vs-agy-opus-high-20261020.md) (10-20, Chess, `runs/argon-vs-agy-opus-high-20261020-{1,2,3}`). Same models and conditions, 3 games played with alternating colors; Argon scored 2:1 in the new batch and **3:1 across all 4 games** (2 games per color). Combined 4-game ACPL was 44.7 (Argon) vs. 53.3 (Opus) with 10 vs. 12 Lichess blunders; because the games shared 24–32 plies of repeated opening moves, the 4 games are not fully independent samples.

- [Per-Move Thinking Token Comparison in International Chess](results/thinking-tokens-argon-opus-20261025.md) (10-25 offline recomputation across Sep–Oct games). Compares per-move thinking tokens across `gemini-4-argon-high` (`high`), historical `claude-opus-5-5` (`high` / `xhigh` / `max`), and `gpt-6-astra` (`xhigh` / `max`). Across the 3 complete Argon–Opus games (154 moves), Argon averaged 1,405.9 thinking tokens per move (median 786), matching the scale of historical Opus `xhigh` (mean 1,311.6–2,180.5) and well below historical Opus `max` (mean 7,195 / median 2,131.5).

- [Carbon High vs. agy Opus 5.5 High: Four-Game Match & Thinking Token Comparison](results/carbon-vs-agy-opus-high-20261114.md) (11-14, Chess). `agy:gemini-4-carbon` vs. `agy:claude-opus-5-5`, both `high` via `llm-chat → agy` (`1.5.8 acp`, Google subscription quota), independent continuous sessions, `real` protocol, `plain` rationale, standard initial position; 4 color-balanced games (Game 0 sequential, Games 1–3 concurrent). Carbon scored **4:0** (all by checkmate) with weighted ACPL **18.1 vs. 35.4** and **2 vs. 7** Lichess blunders (**3:0** and ACPL **17.7 vs. 35.0** across the 3 games with zero internal `agy` retries), showing fewer blunders and greater opening variety than the October Argon batch while still exhibiting tactical oversights in sharp complications (e.g., `24.f6??` and a missed mate-in-3 in Game 1). Also compares per-move thinking tokens for Carbon High (221 moves: mean **2,724.4**, median **1,489**) and a same-day exploratory `agy:gemini-4-hydrogen` High self-play game (clean 1-try moves: mean **1,327.1**, median **911**), both falling in the historical CLI Opus `xhigh` scale.
